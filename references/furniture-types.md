# Furniture types: sizes, joinery, and what goes wrong

Numbers here are standard practice. A drawing you've approved beats this page.

## Tables (dining, kitchen, desks, side and coffee tables)
- **Heights:** dining and desk 29 to 30 in; kitchen/work 34 to 36; coffee 16 to 19; side/end 22 to 26; console 30 to 32. Writing desk keyboard surface 26 to 28.
- **Knee room:** at least 24 to 25 in from floor to apron bottom at a dining table, and 7 in between seat and apron bottom. Aprons usually 3 to 4 1/2 in tall.
- **Seating:** 24 in of edge per diner (30 in is generous); 12 in of overhang to sit at an end.
- **Top:** 3/4 to 1 1/4 in hardwood. Attached with buttons or figure-8s on all aprons (rule 2 in wood-movement.md).
- **Legs and aprons:** legs 1 3/4 to 3 in square; aprons 3/4 to 1 in thick, set back 1/8 to 1/4 from the leg face. Mortise and tenon, tenon 1/3 apron thickness, 1 to 1 1/4 in long, haunched at the top; two aprons meeting in a leg get mitred tenon ends or offset tenons.
- **Trestles and stretchers:** through tenons and wedges or a drawbored M&T; a trestle top attaches with slotted screws through the cross-member.
- **Goes wrong:** top glued to aprons; leg too thin for the mortises from two faces (keep 1/4 in of wood behind each); apron tenons too wide; no room under the apron.

## Chairs, stools, benches
- **Seat height:** dining chair 17 to 18 in; bench 17 to 19; counter stool 24 to 26 (36 in counter); bar stool 28 to 30 (40 to 42 in bar); 10 to 12 in between seat and counter/bar top.
- **Seat:** 15 to 18 in deep, 16 to 20 in wide at the front, sloping back 3 to 5 degrees. Back reclines 95 to 105 degrees from the seat; overall height 30 to 34 in. Arms 7 to 9 in above the seat.
- **Joinery:** chairs rack hard. Mortise and tenon everywhere (tenons at least 1 in long, drawbored or with a long glue line), angled joints cut to the actual leg angle, stretchers low. Windsor/stick chairs: round tenons, wedged at right angles to the seat grain. Never dowels alone or butt joints with screws.
- **Goes wrong:** shoulders that don't follow the angled leg (drawn as a taper down to the tenon instead of cut to the leg angle); short tenons; legs too thin where two mortises meet; seat glued across the grain of the rails.

## Casework (chests of drawers, cabinets, sideboards, bookcases)
- **Sizes:** dresser 30 to 36 in tall, 18 to 22 deep; sideboard 32 to 36 tall, 16 to 20 deep; bookcase shelves 10 to 12 in deep, 9 to 13 in apart.
- **Box:** sides vertical grain; top and bottom side to side; half-blind or through dovetails, or a dado with a rabbet. Back in a rabbet: shiplap, a frame and panel, or ply.
- **Web frames and runners:** front rail tenoned or sliding-dovetailed into the sides; runners tenoned into the rail, glued at the front only, slotted screw or loose tenon at the back (rule 5).
- **Shelves:** 3/4 in solid hardwood sags under books beyond about 30 to 36 in of span (oak, maple); add a front edge strip or go thicker; fixed shelves in stopped dados; adjustable on shelf pins (holes drawn, 1/4 in or 5 mm, 32 mm or 1 in apart).
- **Frame-and-panel cases (posts, rails, muntins, floating panels):** check the corner-post neck where two wall grooves meet, thicken rails flush with the posts on the inside only, give muntins long tenons in pockets inside the groove, and keep 1/4 in of web wherever a rail carries both a panel groove and a floor rabbet (joinery-standards.md, "Webs, necks and cheeks").
- **Case on a stand:** a hidden rabbet around the case bottom drops over a tongue on the stand's aprons. No glued-on locating strips. Stand aprons are tenoned into the legs with mortises that start about 1/2 in below the leg top and a haunch on the tenon.
- **Goes wrong:** runners, kickers and mouldings glued across the sides; solid backs glued in; back not reaching the top and bottom; drawers too tight; thin webs between a groove and a rabbet; a corner-post neck under 1/4 in; mortises right at the top of a leg; locating strips glued under a case.

## Drawers
- **Clearances:** 1/16 in total in width (1/32 per side), 1/16 to 1/8 in height; 1/32 behind a flush front.
- **Hand-fitted drawers (the fine-work way):** the box is made a whisker oversize, then planed with a hand plane until it slides in with a piston fit: about 0.005–0.010 in total side clearance at the front, a touch more toward the back, and the sides waxed. Model the drawer at its **final planed size** (the fit you will plane to), and say so in the project notes; if the model shows the oversize blank instead, add a `"fit_allowance"` note for the drawer in the spec and expect cadcheck to report the drawer touching its runners. In winter-built work leave the height looser, because the case and drawer front move in height.
- **Drawer fronts in a flush case:** fitted the same way, planed to an even gap (about the thickness of a business card to 1/32 in) with a slight back bevel on the ends so the front enters cleanly.
- **Parts:** sides 1/2 to 5/8 in (5/16 to 3/8 in on small drawers under about 4 in tall; secondary wood fine); front half-blind dovetails (or rabbet + dovetail for lipped); back through dovetails or sliding dovetail, sitting on top of the bottom.
- **Bottom:** solid 1/2 in bevelled into a 1/4 in groove, grain side to side, slotted screw at the back; or 1/4 in ply glued in. A glued-up or ripped-and-flipped bottom stays flatter but moves the same total width: leave the full room (rule 10 in wood-movement.md).
- **Drawer between tapered or splayed legs (a drawer in a stand or table base):** the opening narrows toward the narrow end of the taper. Fit the drawer to the narrowest point (planed piston fit there) and guide it with runners whose inner faces sit on the leg-face line, so it can't wander into the wider part. Housing: low aprons tenoned into the legs on three sides, front rails laid flat and tenoned top and bottom, runners and kickers glued to the low aprons (both grains run front to back, so full-length glue is fine), 1/32 clear of the legs.
- **No-rail drawer in a stand (Krenov style):** leave out the front rails; the drawer front fills the whole opening (about 1/32 reveal top and bottom) and the low side and back aprons, runners and kickers do the work. Hide the runners and kickers behind the front. Model the drawer at its planed piston fit and check the drawer back stops about 1/32 off the back apron.
- **Pulls on tangs:** metal tangs or posts on a pull must reach into both the pull and the wood. A tang that stops short of either is holding nothing; run cadcheck's `one_sided` check.
- **Goes wrong:** bottom grain front to back (it then pushes the sides apart); bottom glued; butt-jointed fronts.

## Doors and frame-and-panel
- Stiles run full height, rails between. Tenons 1/3 thickness, 1 to 1 1/4 in long, haunched to fill the groove. Groove 1/4 x 3/8 to 1/2.
- Panels float with room per wood-movement.md; raised panel edge 1/4 thick at the groove.
- **Grain and figure:** side and door panels are plain-sawn with the **cathedral centred** and **straight grain at both edges**; book-match or slip-match where two panels sit side by side. Stiles, rails, muntins, aprons and legs are straight grain (rift or quartered; on legs, rift so all four faces look alike). Choose and mark this at the bench from the full-size printouts, and model and render it the same way.
- Slab doors and tops: breadboard ends (glue middle only, elongated peg holes) or battens in sliding dovetails.
- Inset doors: 1/16 in reveal all round; hinge mortises drawn to the hinge size.

## Beds
- **Mattresses (inside rail dimensions + 1/2 in):** twin 38 x 75; twin XL 38 x 80; full 54 x 75; queen 60 x 80; king 76 x 80; California king 72 x 84.
- Mattress top 22 to 25 in from the floor. Side rails 1 to 1 1/2 in thick, 6 to 10 in tall, with a ledger (grain parallel, glued) for slats.
- **Slats:** 3/4 to 1 in thick, no more than 3 in apart; centre rail with a leg for queen and wider.
- **Rails to posts:** bed bolts (with a stub tenon or dowels to locate) or knock-down bed-rail fasteners, so it comes apart. Headboard panel floats in the posts.
- **Goes wrong:** headboard glued solid into the posts; rails glued in so it can't come apart; slats too far apart.

## Naming for cadcheck (any piece)
- Floating parts: name them `...Panel...`, `Drawer_Bottom`, `Back_Board_n`, `Slat_n` (or set `"role": "floating"` in the spec).
- Fasteners that allow movement: `Button_n`, `Clip_n`, `Figure8_n`, `Cleat`.
- Loose joinery: `...Tenon...`, `Dowel`, `Spline`, `Tongue`, `Peg`, `Wedge`.
- Hardware: `Bolt`, `Screw`, `Hinge`, `Pin`, `Pull`, `Knob`.
- Plywood: `...Ply...` (or `"role": "ply"`).
